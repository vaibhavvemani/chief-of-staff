# Coffee Making Practice — Self-Assessment

Instructions: Answer all five questions. This assessment checks your understanding of coffee making practice and your ability to apply it to real decisions. Questions 1–2 check recall, question 3 checks explanation, and questions 4–5 check application and evaluation.

**Q1. (Recall)** List the four practical decisions that are entirely in your hands each time you brew, according to this session.

**Q2. (Recall)** Name at least three of the popular brewing methods discussed in the session.

**Q3. (Explanation)** The session recommends beginning your coffee learning journey with the agriculture of the coffee plant before moving to grinding, brewing, and roasting. Explain why this order is recommended.

**Q4. (Application)** You are new to coffee and want to brew at home without buying expensive gear, and you want a method that is forgiving while you learn. Which brewing method fits best, and what connected chain of controllable decisions would you follow to make good coffee with it?

**Q5. (Evaluation)** A friend says they want a stronger, more concentrated cup and are willing to invest in equipment and spend time learning. Recommend a method for them and justify your choice against their stated constraints. Contrast it with a more forgiving option.

## Solution

# Answer Key — Coffee Making Practice Self-Assessment (Teacher Only)

**Q1 — Model Answer (Recall).** The four controllable decisions each time you brew are: (1) choosing a brewing method, (2) grinding the beans, (3) storing your coffee, and (4) keeping equipment clean. Full credit for naming all four; partial credit for three (claim cl1, source live_5).

**Q2 — Model Answer (Recall).** Any three of: drip, pour-over, espresso, French press, cold brew. Full credit for three or more correctly named (claim cl2, source live_5).

**Q3 — Model Answer (Explanation).** The order begins with the coffee plant and its agriculture because the way coffee is harvested and processed plays a major role in how coffee should be roasted and brewed. Understanding origin and processing means you stop treating brewing as random steps and instead respond to the coffee in front of you. After the plant, the natural next steps are grinding, brewing, and roasting. Look for the causal link: agriculture/processing → informs roasting and brewing decisions (claim cl3, source live_6; supporting idea in cl6, source live_6).

**Q4 — Model Answer (Application).** The French press fits best because it is a popular brewing method that is forgiving, inexpensive, and does not require specialized equipment. The connected chain of decisions: choose the French press (method), focus on the grind and adjust it if cups taste too weak or too strong, store the beans carefully to protect flavor, and clean the press after each use so old flavors do not linger. Full credit requires naming French press AND describing the method → grind → storage → cleaning chain (claims cl4, cl1, cl5; sources live_5).

**Q5 — Model Answer (Evaluation).** Recommend espresso, since the friend wants a stronger, more concentrated cup and is willing to invest in equipment and learning; espresso requires more specialized equipment and brings together an understanding of the beans and their preparation, drawing a link between preparation methods and flavor profile. Contrast with the French press, which is forgiving and inexpensive but yields a less concentrated result and is better for a beginner who wants low cost and low effort. Full credit requires a justified match of espresso to the stated constraints (investment + concentration) and a contrast with a more forgiving option (claims cl4, cl6, cl2; sources live_5, live_6).
