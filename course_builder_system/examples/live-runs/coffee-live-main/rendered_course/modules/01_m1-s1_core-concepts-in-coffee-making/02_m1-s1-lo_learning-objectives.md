## Content

After studying this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Describe coffee as an agricultural product that travels from seed to cup, explaining how the stages of growing, harvesting, and roasting each shape the flavor in your final cup.
2. Define core coffee vocabulary in your own words, including roast, decaf, organic coffee, and storage and shelf life, and explain how the bean records the effects of extraction, drying, roasting, and storage on taste, texture, and caffeine.
3. Identify and distinguish common brewing methods—drip, pour-over, espresso, French press, and cold brew—and summarize the practical tradeoff each represents for a new coffee maker.
4. Explain the connection between preparation and flavor, using espresso as an example of how a preparation method links directly to a flavor profile.
5. Apply the core concepts to a realistic purchasing and brewing decision by selecting a roast, planning appropriate storage, and matching a brewing method to a stated personal goal such as convenience, control, or intensity.
6. Justify why beginning with the agriculture of coffee is useful, explaining how the way coffee is harvested and processed informs how it should be roasted and brewed.
