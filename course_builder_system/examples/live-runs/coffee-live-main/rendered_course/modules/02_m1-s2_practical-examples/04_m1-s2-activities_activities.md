# Practical Examples: Hands-On Activities

These activities help you turn coffee vocabulary into real, everyday decisions. Each one asks you to trace a short story from bean to cup and to justify the choices you make along the way. Work through them in order, since each builds on the one before.

---

## Activity 1: Map Your Bean Decisions (Solo, ~7 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice connecting bean-handling knowledge to a real buying and storage decision.

**Why it matters:** Coffee in your cup begins on a tree and is grown, harvested, and roasted before you ever see it. Beans are extracted, dried, roasted, and stored, and each of these steps plays a role in coffee's taste, texture, and caffeination. That means your buying and storage choices shape the final cup.

**Steps:**
1. Imagine you are buying coffee for the week ahead.
2. Write down three decisions you would make about the beans. Consider roast level, whether you want decaf or organic, and how you will handle storage and shelf life.
3. For each decision, add one sentence explaining how it connects to taste, texture, or freshness.
4. Note one storage choice you will make to keep the coffee fresh, such as where you will keep it.

**Deliverable:** A short three-row list of decisions, each paired with a one-sentence justification, plus one storage note.

**Completion criterion:** Every decision is tied to a reason about how bean handling affects the final cup.

---

## Activity 2: Match a Brewing Method to Your Morning (Solo, ~7 minutes)

**Purpose:** Practice choosing a brewing method that fits your situation rather than memorizing methods for their own sake.

**Why it matters:** Popular brewing methods include drip, pour-over, espresso, French press, and cold brew. The same beans can become very different drinks depending on the method and technique you choose, so the right question is: given my time, my equipment, and the taste I want, which method fits?

**Steps:**
1. Describe your typical weekday morning in two or three sentences. Include how much time you have and what equipment you own.
2. List your three constraints: time available, equipment on hand, and the taste or experience you want.
3. From the popular methods (drip, pour-over, espresso, French press, cold brew), pick the one that best fits.
4. Write two sentences explaining why that method fits your constraints better than one alternative you rejected.

**Deliverable:** A short paragraph naming your chosen method, your three constraints, and your reasoning against one rejected method.

**Completion criterion:** Your choice is clearly justified by at least two of your stated constraints.

---

## Activity 3: Trace the Whole Decision Chain (Pair or Solo, ~6 minutes)

**Purpose:** Bring both examples together into one connected bean-to-cup story.

**Why it matters:** Origin, bean-handling steps, and brewing methods are not separate facts. They are stages in one connected decision chain that ends with the cup you want.

**Steps:**
1. Using your answers from Activities 1 and 2, write a short "story" of one cup of coffee: start with the bean you chose, move through your storage decision, and finish with your brewing method.
2. Mark the exact point where each decision was made.
3. If working with a partner, swap stories and ask each other one question about a decision.
4. When you are unsure which roast or method fits a goal, note that a trusted consumer resource developed for consumers, students, and others can help you explore origins, beans, roasts, and brewing methods rather than guessing.

**Deliverable:** A single connected paragraph tracing your coffee from bean to cup with each decision point labeled.

**Completion criterion:** Your story shows a clear chain from origin and bean handling through to a brewing method, with reasoning at each stage.
