# Coffee Making Troubleshooting: Practice Activities

These activities let you practice diagnosing a disappointing cup and choosing a single, deliberate change to try next. Work through them in order. Each one builds the core troubleshooting habit: run through the variables you control, change one thing at a time, and taste again.

---

## Activity 1: Build Your Troubleshooting Checklist

**Purpose:** Turn the four things you can actually control into a quick, reusable checklist you can run through whenever a cup disappoints.

**Time:** About 4 minutes.

**Grouping:** Individual.

**Steps:**

1. On paper or a note app, write the four practical levers you control at home: the beans and how they are stored, the grind, the brewing method, and how you clean and maintain your equipment.
2. Next to each lever, write one short question you would ask yourself to check it (for example, for beans: "Are these fresh and well stored?").
3. Order the four items the way the lesson recommends working through a bad cup, from the first sensible check to the last logical suspect.
4. Add a one-line reminder at the bottom about how many variables to change at a time.

**Deliverable:** A four-item ordered checklist, each with a diagnostic question, plus your one-line rule about changing variables. You are done when your list moves from beans and storage through to cleaning and states the one-variable rule.

---

## Activity 2: Diagnose the Weak, Watery Cup

**Purpose:** Practice tracing a specific fault backward through the chain to a likely cause.

**Time:** About 5 minutes.

**Grouping:** Individual or pairs.

**Scenario:** A pot of drip coffee tastes thin and slightly sour. The beans are about a week old and stored in a sealed container.

**Steps:**

1. Run through your checklist from Activity 1 in order.
2. For each lever, write "likely fine" or "suspect," and briefly say why. Consider what a grind that is too coarse tends to do to a cup.
3. Identify the single most likely cause based on your reasoning.
4. State the one change you would make before the next pot, and note what you will keep the same.

**Deliverable:** A short written diagnosis naming one suspected cause and one specific single change, with everything else held constant. You are done when your answer names exactly one change and explains why the other levers were ruled out.

---

## Activity 3: Match the Fix to the Method

**Purpose:** Practice choosing an adjustment that fits the brewing method rather than blaming the beans.

**Time:** About 5 minutes.

**Grouping:** Pairs or small groups.

**Scenario:** A French press comes out harsh and bitter. The beans are fresh, and the maker has been grinding fine because that worked well for espresso.

**Steps:**

1. As a pair, name which brewing method is in use and describe, in one sentence, how it brews compared with espresso.
2. Discuss what a grind that is too fine tends to do during a long steep.
3. Agree on the single change that best matches this method.
4. Write one sentence explaining why this is a better first move than replacing the beans.

**Deliverable:** A shared written recommendation of one method-appropriate change plus your one-sentence justification. You are done when your recommendation names one change and ties it to how the method brews.

---

## Activity 4: Bring Your Own Bad Cup

**Purpose:** Apply the full troubleshooting path to a real cup from your own life.

**Time:** About 6 minutes.

**Grouping:** Individual, shared aloud if in a group.

**Steps:**

1. Recall a recent cup of coffee that disappointed you, or describe the next one you plan to make. Note the brewing method you used or will use.
2. Describe the flaw in a few words (for example, weak, watery, sour, bitter, or "off").
3. Work backward through beans and storage, grind, method, and cleaning, marking each as likely fine or suspect.
4. Choose the one variable you would change first, and predict what should happen to the taste.
5. Write a one-line reflection on what you would learn from the result whether or not the change works.

**Deliverable:** A written mini-plan for one real or upcoming cup: the flaw, your ordered check, one chosen change, your prediction, and your reflection. You are done when your plan commits to a single change and states what you will observe next.

---

## Wrap-Up Reflection

In one or two sentences, describe how changing only one variable at a time makes it easier to learn from each cup. Keep this note with your checklist so you can reuse the whole process next time a cup disappoints.
