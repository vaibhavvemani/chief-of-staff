## Content

By the end of this subtopic, you will be able to:

1. Define coffee making troubleshooting as the practice of noticing what went wrong and adjusting one variable at a time until the cup improves.
2. Explain why the same coffee problem can have several causes by describing coffee preparation as a connected chain in which each stage—such as how beans are extracted, dried, roasted, and stored—affects the next.
3. List the practical variables a home brewer can control, including the beans and their storage, the grind, the brewing method, and equipment cleaning.
4. Apply an ordered troubleshooting sequence—beans and storage, grind, method, then cleaning—changing only one variable at a time and tasting again.
5. Diagnose a weak, watery, or sour cup by relating a too-coarse grind to water passing through too quickly, and a bitter, harsh cup to a too-fine grind that over-extracts.
6. Match a troubleshooting adjustment to the brewing method in use, distinguishing among common methods such as drip, pour-over, espresso, French press, and cold brew.
7. Identify when stale oils and residue from uncleaned equipment are the likely cause of persistent off-flavors that grind and freshness cannot explain.
8. Decide on the single most sensible change to make before the next cup, and justify that choice using the troubleshooting chain.
