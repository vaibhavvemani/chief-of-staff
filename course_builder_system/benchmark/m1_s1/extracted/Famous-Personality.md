FAMOUS PERSONALITY
Frank Knight — The Foundation of Risk Theory

"In 1921, economist Frank Knight introduced a distinction that still underpins modern Financial Risk Management. Knight argued that risk and uncertainty are not the same."

Risk vs. Uncertainty

Risk
Situations where probabilities can be assigned. For example, we can estimate the volatility of exchange rates or default probabilities using historical data.

Uncertainty
Situations where no reliable probability distribution exists. These are the "unknown unknowns" — beyond the reach of quantitative modelling.

Why Does This Matter?
Financial Risk Management operates only in the domain of measurable risk. Our models — VaR, stress testing, credit models — all assume probability estimation is possible.

Model Boundaries
Why Profit Exists
Philosophical Foundation
Knight's distinction clarifies what our quantitative tools can and cannot capture.
If everything were measurable and perfectly predictable, there would be no excess return. Profit compensates for uncertainty.
This conceptual distinction forms the philosophical bedrock of the entire discipline.
