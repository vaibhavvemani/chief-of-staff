The central question: Why did Lehman fail?

Business Model

Lehman operated heavily in mortgage-backed securities. It borrowed short-term funds from the repo market and invested in long-term, illiquid mortgage assets.
The Maturity Mismatch
Short-term liabilities.Long-term assets.
This created a critical structural vulnerability at the heart of its balance sheet.
Funding that could vanish overnight — tied to assets that could not be sold quickly.

Good Times
Bad Times
High leverage magnifies returns — a small gain on assets produces outsized equity returns.
High leverage equally magnifies losses — a small decline in asset value can wipe out equity entirely.

Market Risk

When housing prices began declining, the value of mortgage-backed securities fell. That directly exposed Lehman to market risk — the risk that asset values move against a firm's positions.
Key Insight
Market risk alone does not cause collapse.
It was the combination of risks that proved fatal.
Losses mounted on the balance sheet as the housing market deteriorated through 2007 and into 2008.

Counterparties began to lose confidence in Lehman's balance sheet as losses mounted.
Short-term lenders refused to roll over funding — the repo market dried up almost overnight.
Without short-term funding, Lehman could not operate — despite holding long-term assets.

Risk Management Failure
The firm had models. It had VaR calculations. It had internal controls. Yet three critical failures occurred:

Excessive Leverage
1
Leverage exceeded the firm's capacity to absorb losses under stress scenarios.

Tail Risk Underestimated
2
Models failed to account for extreme, low-probability but high-impact events.

Liquidity Dependency
3
Over-reliance on short-term funding with no adequate contingency plan.

This was not a failure of mathematics. It was a failure of risk alignment.

Teaching Transition
Lehman demonstrates that risk exists in layers — and that each layer interacts with the others:

Market Risk
Liquidity Risk
Governance Failure
Triggered losses as housing prices declined and MBS values fell.
Accelerated collapse as short-term funding was withdrawn by counterparties.
Amplified exposure by allowing excessive leverage and poor risk alignment.

"This is why Financial Risk Management is not optional — it is structural."
