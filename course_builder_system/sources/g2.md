## Curated Source Notes

The Basel Committee defines credit risk around failure by a borrower or
counterparty to meet agreed obligations.

Key grounding points:

- Credit risk is the potential that a borrower or counterparty will fail to
  meet obligations under agreed terms.
- Credit risk is not limited to loans. It can arise in loans, bonds,
  interbank transactions, trade finance, foreign exchange, futures, swaps,
  options, guarantees, commitments, and settlement activity.
- Credit risk management aims to keep exposure within acceptable parameters
  while pursuing risk-adjusted return.
- Banks need to manage credit risk at both the individual-transaction level
  and the portfolio level.
- Credit risk interacts with other risks, including liquidity, market,
  operational, and reputational risk.

Short quoted anchor:

> "fail to meet its obligations in accordance with agreed terms"

## Claims This Source Can Support

- Credit risk is the risk of loss from borrower or counterparty failure.
- Credit risk exists beyond ordinary loans.
- Credit risk management requires identifying, measuring, monitoring, and
  controlling exposure.
- Settlement risk can include credit, liquidity, market, operational, and
  reputational elements.
