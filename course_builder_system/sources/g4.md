## Curated Source Notes

This file supports two core risk categories that m1_s1 introduces: market risk
and operational risk.

Market risk grounding points:

- Market risk is associated with trading activity and changes in market risk
  factors.
- Market risk frameworks distinguish between trading book and banking book
  exposures.
- Basel's revised market risk framework uses expected shortfall for internal
  models and includes attention to non-modellable risk factors.
- Market risk can include interest rate risk, foreign exchange risk, credit
  spread risk, equity risk, commodity risk, and market illiquidity effects.
- Market risk is continuous because prices, rates, spreads, and volatilities
  can move frequently.

Operational risk grounding points:

- Operational risk covers loss from internal process failures, people,
  systems, or external events.
- Legal risk is commonly included inside operational risk.
- Operational risk is different from market and credit risk because it comes
  from execution, control, systems, people, or external-event failures rather
  than from ordinary price movement or borrower default.

Short quoted anchors:

> "trading book and the banking book"

> "people and systems or from external events"

## Claims This Source Can Support

- Market risk comes from changes in market factors affecting positions.
- Interest rates, foreign exchange, credit spreads, equities, commodities, and
  liquidity conditions are market-risk drivers.
- Operational risk arises from failed processes, people, systems, or external
  events.
- Operational risk is a distinct core financial-risk category.
- Market risk and operational risk require different measurement and control
  approaches.
