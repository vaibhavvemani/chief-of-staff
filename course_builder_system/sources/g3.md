## Curated Source Notes

The Basel Committee frames liquidity as the ability of a bank to fund asset
growth and meet obligations when due without unacceptable losses. This is
central to explaining why a solvent-looking firm can still fail if funding
disappears.

Key grounding points:

- Liquidity is the ability to fund increases in assets and meet obligations
  as they come due without unacceptable losses.
- Banks are naturally exposed to liquidity risk because they often transform
  short-term funding into longer-term assets.
- Liquidity risk can be institution-specific or market-wide.
- A liquidity shortfall at one institution can create system-wide effects.
- The 2007-08 turmoil showed that liquidity can disappear quickly and remain
  unavailable for a long period.
- Sound liquidity risk management includes liquidity risk tolerance, liquid
  asset cushions, stress testing, contingency funding plans, intraday
  liquidity management, collateral management, and public disclosure.

Short quoted anchor:

> "meet obligations as they come due"

## Claims This Source Can Support

- Liquidity risk is about meeting cash flow obligations on time.
- Liquidity risk is not identical to insolvency.
- Liquidity problems can spread through the financial system.
- Funding stress can be sudden, especially when markets lose confidence.
- Stress tests and contingency funding plans are part of sound liquidity
  risk management.
