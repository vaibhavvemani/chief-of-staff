## Curated Source Notes

The Financial Crisis Inquiry Report is the main grounding source for the Lehman
case. It provides official narrative and factual support for using Lehman as a
multi-risk case: market risk, liquidity risk, leverage, funding dependence,
asset valuation concerns, and risk governance failure.

Key grounding points:

- Lehman was a central event in the September 2008 financial crisis.
- Lehman's problems involved both solvency concerns and liquidity concerns.
- Investors and regulators focused on whether Lehman had enough real capital
  after possible asset write-downs and enough cash to withstand a run.
- At the end of Q1 2008, the report identifies concerns about Lehman's
  real-estate-related investments and short-term funding reliance.
- The report cites $7.8 billion of commercial paper and $197 billion of repos
  at the end of Q1 2008.
- Lehman relied heavily on repo funding, including overnight funding secured
  by illiquid assets.
- As of mid-June 2008, 62% of Lehman's liquidity depended on borrowing against
  nontraditional securities such as illiquid mortgage-related securities.
- The report describes tri-party repo exposure of roughly $200 billion shortly
  before failure.
- The report also discusses "Repo 105" transactions and concerns that they
  reduced reported balance-sheet size around reporting dates.
- The Office of Thrift Supervision warned in July 2008 that Lehman was
  materially overexposed to commercial real estate and had major failings in
  its risk management process.

Short quoted anchors:

> "Solvency and liquidity were essential and related."

> "materially overexposed"

## Claims This Source Can Support

- Lehman illustrates interaction between market risk, liquidity risk, leverage,
  and governance/risk-management failure.
- Short-term repo funding made Lehman vulnerable to confidence loss.
- Illiquid mortgage-related exposures were central to Lehman's funding and
  valuation problems.
- Lehman's case is not just about one risk type; it is a layered-risk failure.
- Liquidity stress can force asset sales and worsen solvency concerns.
